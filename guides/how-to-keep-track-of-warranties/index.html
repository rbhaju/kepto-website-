How to keep track of warranties.

Updated 22 November 2026

Most warranties are lost in the gap between buying a thing and it breaking. The receipt fades, the box goes out, and nobody remembers whether the washing machine was two years or five. A little structure fixes that.

The short version
  • Record five things for each product, on the day you buy it.
  • Set a reminder about a month before the warranty ends.
  • Keep the receipt copy with the record, not in a separate pile.
  • Check the product over when the reminder arrives.

What to record

  • What it is, with the model if it has one
  • Where and when you bought it
  • What you paid
  • How long the warranty runs, and any extended warranty you added
  • A copy of the receipt

The serial number is worth adding for anything expensive. It is usually on a sticker on the back or underside.

When to be reminded

A reminder on the last day is too late. Set it about 30 days before the warranty ends. That leaves time to test the product properly, book a repair and send it off while it is still covered.

When the reminder arrives, run through the things that fail quietly: battery life, door seals, hinges, buttons, any noise that was not there before.

Where to keep it

A folder or shoebox works until the receipts fade, and it cannot remind you of anything.

A spreadsheet can hold the dates, but you have to remember to open it, and the receipt photos live somewhere else.

A warranty tracker app keeps the receipt, the dates and the reminder together. This is what Kepto was built for.

Other dates worth tracking the same way

  • the return window on a new purchase
  • services, such as a car service or an air conditioner clean
  • car registration and insurance renewals
  • passport and licence expiry
