How to keep track of subscriptions.

Updated 25 November 2026

Subscriptions are easy to start and easy to forget. A free trial becomes $14.99 a month, a yearly plan renews on a day you had no reason to remember, and a year later you are paying for three things you do not use.

The short version
  • List every subscription once, from your bank and card statements.
  • Write down the price, how often it renews and the next renewal date.
  • Set a reminder a few days before each renewal, and before a free trial ends.
  • Look over the list every few months and cancel what you do not use.

Find them all

Go through three months of bank and card statements and note every charge that repeats. Then check the places subscriptions hide:

  • the Subscriptions page in your Apple or Google account
  • PayPal automatic payments
  • your email, searched for "renewal", "receipt" and "your subscription"
  • yearly charges, which a three-month look will miss, so check a full year for those

What to write down

  • The name, and which account or card pays for it
  • The price and how often: weekly, monthly or yearly
  • The next renewal date
  • Whether it is a free trial, and the day the trial ends
  • How to cancel it, if that was hard to find

Turn each price into a yearly figure. $14.99 a month is about $180 a year, and a number that size is easier to judge.

Be reminded before, not after

A reminder on renewal day is too late, since the charge has usually gone through. Set it a few days before, and a day or two before a free trial ends.

The ACCC has warned about subscription traps, where a sign-up is easy and cancelling is not. Before you enter card details for a trial, check the price after the trial, the renewal date and how you cancel. If you do cancel, keep the confirmation.

Review a few times a year

Every three or four months, read down the list and ask one question of each line: did I use this in the last month? If not, cancel it. You can usually sign up again later.
