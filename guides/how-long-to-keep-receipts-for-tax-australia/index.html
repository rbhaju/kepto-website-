How long to keep receipts for tax in Australia.

Updated 22 November 2026

If you claim work expenses, run a side business or own a rental, the receipt is your evidence. Here is how long to keep it, what it has to show, and why a photo is usually the safer copy.

The short version
  • Keep records for five years from the date you lodge your tax return.
  • A clear photo or scan of a receipt is accepted in place of the paper.
  • The receipt needs the supplier, the amount, what was bought and the date.
  • Thermal paper fades, often within a year or two, so copy receipts early.

How long to keep them

The Australian Taxation Office says most people need to keep their records for five years from the date they lodge the tax return the records relate to. Some records run longer. For an asset you depreciate, or something you may pay capital gains tax on, the five years starts later, so keep those records for as long as you own the asset and then five years more.

The official guidance is on the ATO website under Records you need to keep.

What a receipt must show

For an expense you want to claim, the record should show:

  • the name of the supplier
  • the amount paid
  • what the goods or services were
  • the date you paid
  • the date of the document

A bank statement line alone often misses what was bought, which is why the receipt itself matters.

Is a photo of a receipt enough?

Yes. The ATO accepts digital copies, including photos, as long as they are a true and clear copy of the original and you can show them if asked. Once you have a clear copy you do not need to keep the paper for tax purposes.

Check the photo before you throw the paper away. The whole receipt should be in frame and every line should be readable.

Why paper receipts fail

Most shop receipts are printed on thermal paper. Heat, sunlight and time turn them blank, and a glovebox or a wallet speeds that up. A receipt you can no longer read is not much use five years later, so the day you get it is the best day to copy it.

A simple routine

  1. Photograph the receipt the day you get it.
  2. Note whether it is for work, and what share of the use is for work.
  3. Keep the copies somewhere that is backed up, not only in your camera roll.
  4. At tax time, pull out the year's claims in one go.
